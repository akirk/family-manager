# Households

A WordPress app powered by [WpApp](https://github.com/akirk/wp-app) for running a household — or several. Most family apps assume one home; this one is built for the families that have more than one.

[Try Households in WordPress Playground](https://playground.wordpress.net/?blueprint-url=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akirk/households/main/blueprint.json) · [Try it with demo data](https://playground.wordpress.net/?blueprint-url=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akirk/households/main/demo.json)

[Try it in OpenStation](https://playground.wordpress.net/?blueprint-url=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/akirk/households/main/blueprint-openstation.json) — the same app opened in desktop mode with the [OpenStation](https://github.com/WordPress/openstation) plugin.

## How it works

- **It is a WordPress plugin, not an app that happens to live in WordPress.** Every page is server-rendered PHP: it reads through `Storage`, prints its lists, and every change is a `<form method="post">` posting back to the page it was made on. A handler on `template_redirect` checks the nonce, does the work and redirects to the same URL, so a refresh repeats nothing and what comes back is simply the page read afresh; anything refused is named in the URL and said by the page. There is no build step, no endpoint the pages talk to, and nothing that needs JavaScript to work. Two places use a little of it and neither needs it: a `confirm()` on removing someone from a home, which without it simply submits; and the whereabouts board, which posts its own forms and puts back the sections the server rendered in reply, so tapping a run of days or paging to the fortnight after does not send the page away and bring it back. Paging is shown as the days moving past rather than the board being replaced: whose row is whose does not change from one fortnight to the next, so only the day columns of the fortnight asked for are taken out of what the server sent and hung on the ends of the rows already there, out of sight beyond the edge. The board is then twice as wide as its window, and the window is scrolled the width of a fortnight — real scrolling, so the names hold their place at the left exactly as they do when you drag the days across by hand. When it comes to rest the fortnight that has gone by is deleted and the days that scrolled into place are left where they are: they are the very cells that arrived, never redrawn, so nothing settles at the end. The scrollbar is held still for the length of it, because a bar appearing while the board is briefly twice as wide, and going again when it is not, is the one thing on screen that would give away that anything had been loaded. Anyone who has asked their system for less movement is simply put down at the other end. Both scripts make the same request the browser would have made on its own, so switching them off changes how the pages feel and not what they do.

- **A home is a term; everything in it is a post tagged with that term.** People, the facts a house needs you to know, the things kept there, what needs doing — one join answers every question about a home, and a question about several homes at once is the same join with more terms in it. The taxonomy is registered closed, because terms are global in a way private posts are not.

  | | |
  |---|---|
  | `/households/` | the overview: what the household you are in asks of you, what is kept there, and where everyone is |
  | `/households/homes/` | every home you belong to, and where a new one is started |
  | `/households/<id>/` | one home: its people, tasks, facts and things |
  | `/households/<id>/manage/` | who is in it, who administers it, what it is called |
  | `/households/<id>/as/<person id>/` | that home as one of its people sees it |
  | `/households/where/` | everyone across your homes, where they are today, and the fortnight ahead |
  | `/households/things/` | everything kept across your homes, and which houses keep it |
  | `/households/pack/` | what is waiting to be taken from one home to another, and the trip it is waiting for |
  | `/households/person/<person id>/` | a person, and what travels with them between homes |

  Ask for a home you do not belong to and you are sent back to the ones you do.

  A home is started from `/households/homes/`, which settles the one thing a home cannot be without: someone who may add to it. Whoever starts it administers it and lands on its page to say who is in it — themselves included, with one button, because setting a household up is not the same as living in it. Somebody has to make the grandparents' house before anybody is put in it. Reaching a home is therefore being in it *or* administering it, and someone who already has a record joins with it — a second home does not make a second person. The name is not made unique: terms are global in a way families are not, so two households on one site may both have a "Home", and being refused — or told the other exists — would be wrong twice over. The slug is made unique instead, and the name is left alone.

- **The front page is about you, not about your houses.** A list of homes is a directory; what a person opens the app to ask is what today wants from them. So the overview answers that, in two columns, both of them read from the household you are standing in. On the left is what that house asks: everything still open in it — everybody's, because a household's list is not a private one, each line saying whose it is or that it is nobody's and there for the claiming — and then the things kept there, said the way that house says them, because it is the shelf you can actually reach. Whose it is is a filter in the corner rather than a heading, and a `+` beside it writes something down without leaving for the household's own page. Both live in the URL, so a tick does not close the form and a filtered list can be handed to somebody else. On the right is where everyone is: your households a line each, the one you are at filled in rather than said in a sentence, and under them the fortnight ahead as one dated list. The appointments, the handovers and the birthdays are merged rather than kept in three sections, because a day is one thing to the person living it even when it is spread across three houses.

  Nothing says where you are and both left-hand sections say so rather than guessing: there is no household to read from, and the way to fix that is on the right.

  The section headings carry the links out. Each is the name of the page it opens — *Your households*, *Who is where* — and the things section keeps its across-the-houses link in its corner, so the page ends at its last line instead of at a row of links repeating what the headings already say.

  Moving is done here rather than deferred to the board. Every household folds open to a list of the people you may say a day for — yourself, and anyone in a household you organise — as checkboxes, with you already ticked, so a parent taking two children to the grandparents' is one tick each and one press. It is a statement about today alone and sets up no arrangement: the pattern is untouched and answers again tomorrow. Naming nobody says it about you, which is what the plain buttons elsewhere post, and the whole party is checked before any of it is written down, because half of them arriving is worse than none. When nothing can say where you are — you belong to several homes and rotate between none — no household is marked and the page says so rather than shrugging.

- **A person is a post, and the WordPress account is optional.** The record is the person; the login is identity attached to it when they actually need to sign in — `post_author`, or nobody at all. That is how a toddler whose shoe size is worth writing down, or a relative who will never log in, gets to exist here without an account they would never use. The plugin makes no accounts: they are made in WordPress, by whoever runs the site, and pointed at a person afterwards from the manage page. One account answers for one person, and an account that stops being someone stops administering their households at the same moment, because administering is held as a user ID.

- **What travels with a person is prose, not fields.** Sizes, allergies, what the next person needs to know — it is the post's own content, so every edit is kept as a revision and a size written down with a date still says something a year later. A field that is silently overwritten hides its own age; a dated line does not.

- **Two decisions, not five roles.** Whether someone *administers a home* is per home and lives in term meta — and administering one is enough to open and organise it, membership or not. Whether someone is a *child* is a property of the person, true wherever they go. Both are asked through the ordinary capability API — `current_user_can( 'manage_household', $home_id )` — so nothing in the plugin invents its own permission language.

- **Who is where** is the overview of people: everyone across the homes you belong to, listed once each — someone in three of your homes is one person, not three entries — with where they are today, then a day-by-day board for the next fortnight and the handovers that follow from it. A row is a name and a household per button, and where someone is is said by which button is pressed rather than repeated in a label beside it. What is true of the person rather than of today — that they are a child, what the family calls them, whether they have an account at all — is on their own page, one tap away under their name, because a list of where everybody is is not where you go to read it. People moving together on the same day are one handover, because that is one trip. It spans homes, so it says which one it is reading from and lets you pick another.
  - Anyone who belongs to more than one home can have a **rotation**: children between separated parents, but equally a week at the grandparents or a share in a holiday house. A rotation names its homes in order — as many as the family uses, not just two — and repeats a cycle of days. Pick week on/week off, 2-2-3, every other weekend, or set the fourteen days yourself.
  - The rotation is stored on the person rather than on any one home, so every home reads the same answer.
  - The board opens on the next fortnight and an arrow either side moves it a fortnight at a time, forward as far as you care to look, so a summer can be arranged in August. Only forward: a day that has been and gone has had what was said about it pruned, and the pattern reciting it backwards would be guessing. Each window is walked from the day before it starts, so a move onto its first day is still a handover — otherwise paging ahead would hide exactly the arrival that made you page. The heading names the window it is showing, and *Back to today* returns in one step from however far out you have gone.
  - A single day is moved by tapping it on the board. That override wins over the pattern and leaves it untouched, so a swapped weekend does not shift every week after it. The same tap says a day for someone who has no pattern at all — and for them it does not stop at the day: with nothing else able to answer for the days that follow, what was said stands until the next day that is said. Somebody who moves on Tuesday is still there on Wednesday. So the fortnight after a move reads as the move — the same household, day after day, up to whatever switch is already arranged after it — rather than as one marked day and then a fortnight of blanks. Only the day that was said is marked, so the marks on the board are the moves themselves.
  - A tap can be made to carry further. Switched to **from that day onwards**, it says the day and clears whatever was already arranged after it in the fortnight, which is how a plan replaces an arrangement instead of threading through it. For someone on a pattern that means saying every remaining day outright, because the pattern would answer for any day that was not; for someone without one it stays a single statement, since that is already what a statement does. Either way the pattern itself is untouched and picks up again where the board ends, and tapping round to *back to the pattern* hands the whole run back. Which of the two a tap means is kept in the URL, so it survives a reload and can be passed to somebody else.

- **Belonging to a home is not being at it, and being at one is how you come to belong.** Saying someone is at a household they are not in yet puts them in it: the first weekend at the grandparents is a move before it is an arrangement. Leaving stays deliberate and separate. A rotation says where someone is. Without one, a person who belongs to a single home is at it, because there is nowhere else they could be — but someone who belongs to several, rotates between none and has never said where they are is not tracked, and rather than claim they are in three places at once, the app asks them. A day said outright needs no pattern behind it to be true, and it wins over anything a cycle works out. What it means for the days after it depends on whether anything else can speak for them: with a pattern behind it, it is a statement about that one day alone, and the cycle takes the next one back; with no pattern, there is nothing to hand them back to, so it stands until the next day that is said. Saying where *you* are is something anyone may do about themselves, child or not; saying it about someone else is organising, and stays with the people who organise.

- **About this home** holds what people ask when they are in a house they do not live in every day: the wifi network, where the water main valve is, which day the bins go out. It belongs to the home because it is true of the place, and everyone there can read it — so it is not the place for anything that should not be shared with all of them.

- **Things kept here** is the same record in another mood: what lives in the house, and where in it. Where it lives is asked once per household rather than once for the thing, because something kept at two houses is somewhere different in each — the spare charger is in the hall drawer at one and beside the bed at the other — and a single line would be wrong at whichever house you were standing in. So a thing names every household that keeps it, each with its own line, and where a fact is one house's and lives in the post's own content, a thing's line is written against the household. Its own page asks every household of yours the same question, one line each — *where should it be here?* — with the answer beside the name rather than under it, and one press for all of them, because it is one answer about one thing rather than a row of separate ones. Each line is still its own household's to write, so each is asked of that household rather than of the one the form happens to name. Answering for a house that has never had the thing is how that house comes to have a place for it. A house told nothing is given nothing, so an empty line hands nothing to anybody; a house that already has a place for it may empty its own line, which says only that nobody has written down where.

  Where a thing **lives** and where it **is** are two questions, and the app keeps them apart. The hook by the door is where it lives whether or not it is hanging there, so every house that keeps it has a line to write and read even while the thing is away. Where it has got to is one mark on the thing itself: it is at whichever household was last said, which may be one that does not keep it — a thing taken along for the weekend is lent, not moved, and goes back by being said to be back. Said nothing at all, a thing kept in one house is in it, and a thing kept in several is somewhere among them nobody has named, which is answered as not known rather than guessed at. Under a household's heading a thing says where it lives in that house and nothing about the other houses that have a place for it: beside something that is in one place at a time, naming them reads as it being in several, which is the other question's to answer. The lists say where it is only where that adds something — under a household's heading, whenever the thing is not there — and a household's own page has a **Here just now** list of what has been brought to it that it does not keep, so a thing on loan is findable at the house it is actually in. A household nobody in the room belongs to is never named: it is somewhere that is not yours, and nothing more. `/households/things/` shows everything across your homes at once, which is where you look when you cannot find the thing; read house by house it appears under each of them, and read as one list it appears once and names them.

- **Where a thing is to get to is a third question, and it is what a bag is for.** Between deciding the swimming kit has to be at the other house and it being there is a bag that has to be packed, and for all of that time the truthful answer to where the thing is is the house it is standing in. So saying it is to go somewhere is a mark beside where it is rather than instead of it: nothing moves, and no line about where it lives is touched.

  Packing it is a third mark again, and it moves nothing either — a thing in a bag by the door is at the house it was always at, and that is still where to look for it. So the bag is ticked off thing by thing and **carried in one press**, at the foot of the list, because carrying it is one thing somebody did rather than a dozen. Only then is what was in it at the other house, and off the list; whatever was not ticked was not taken, and is still there for the next trip that way.

  A tick is not a disappearance, here as anywhere else in the app: what is in the bag stays on the list, struck through, and the same box again takes it back out. A cross takes something off the list altogether, and because a cross is easy to mean and easy to miss by, what it took off is named in the URL and offered back until you navigate away.

  `/households/pack/` is everything that has been said that way, gathered a trip at a time. Things going the same way are one list, because that is one bag, and each list says when the trip is — taken from the moves the fortnight already holds, since the list is the answer to "what goes with us" — or says plainly that nobody is going that way yet and it waits until somebody does. Nobody has said where a thing is at all and it is a list with one end, which is named, rather than a guess at the other. The home it is going to has to be one of yours, and so has one of the houses that keep it — a bag you could do nothing about is not one to hand you. Where it is coming from is named only when that is yours as well: something lent to a house that is not is still waiting to come back and is still on the list, but that house is no more named here than anywhere else.

  A trip that has something waiting says so where the trip is already said: beside the move on the overview, next to *Who is where*, and beside the handover on the board — how much is still to be packed, and the way to the list it is waiting on. The shelf on the overview says it the other way round, thing by thing and on the line the thing is on: an arrow, the house it is to go to, and a tick once it is in the bag. A shelf being read on the way out of the door wants the answer rather than a sentence about the answer, so the words are kept for whoever the arrow does not reach. The lists that can be written in say it the same way, and leave the tick to the box that does the ticking.

  A thing's line is one partial, and so is what can be said about where it is to go, so a household's own page, the things page, the shelf on the overview and the packlist all print the same row. They differ only in what the heading has already said: which household this is, or — on the packlist, where the heading is a trip — both ends of it, which is why a line there says neither.

- **Leaving is not deleting.** Taking someone out of a home removes the membership; the record and everything written on it stays, because a child's history should not evaporate when an arrangement changes.

This started as Family Manager, was renamed to Households, and has since been rebuilt on the model above. There is no upgrade path from either — this is a prototype, so start it fresh.

The demo blueprint (`demo.json`) seeds three homes — two separated parents and a grandparent — with three children who belong to all of them, rotating week on and week off between the parents with a one-off day at the grandparent's. The youngest has no account at all. The member accounts use the password `demo`.
